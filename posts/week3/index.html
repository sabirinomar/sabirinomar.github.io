Centrality gives us several ways to call a character important. But this week we wanted to ask something more physical: if a character disappears, does the network actually notice?

What are we testing?

Centrality gives us several ways to call a node “important”. The game tests something slightly different: structural dependence. If removing a character separates other nodes from the giant component, the network depended on that character in a way that a centrality score alone may not reveal.

Once you've tried it yourself, we repeat the experiment systematically for every character.

04 / Find the outliers

Does being a bridge make you irreplaceable?

Betweenness tells us how often a character lies on shortest paths. Our knockout experiment asks a different question: what actually happens when that character disappears?

Here we put the two measures against each other. Each dot is one character.

→ Further right = higher betweenness: more shortest paths use that character.
→ Further up = greater knockout damage: more characters leave the giant component when they are removed.

If betweenness perfectly predicted structural damage, the two rankings would closely agree. They do not. The interesting characters are the ones that break the pattern.

r = 0.723Strong overall relationshiprank r = 0.306Much weaker agreement on who ranks where

The first number says betweenness and damage generally increase together. The second says they often disagree about WHICH characters matter most.

Look for the outliers: Wolverine has very high betweenness but causes little fragmentation, while Black Widow causes more damage than her betweenness rank alone would suggest.

06 / Compared to what?

200 alternative Marvel universes

Even that is not enough. A character with many links is almost guaranteed to sit on many paths. So we ask one final question: is their brokerage unusual for a character with that degree?

Central compared to what?

Popularity is not brokerage. Brokerage is not robustness.

Spider-Man does the most immediate damage. Wolverine shows that being on many paths does not make you irreplaceable. Rockman shows the opposite kind of surprise: a modest-looking node can occupy a position that almost never appears in networks with the same degrees.

That is why the most interesting number in network science is rarely the number on its own. It is the number compared with the world we would have expected instead.

277 characters. 200 alternative universes. One very breakable web.
