Week 3 · Robustness & centrality

Who holds the Marvel universe together?

A character can be highly connected, close to everyone, or strategically positioned between groups. We asked a more destructive question: what happens to the hyperlink network when one character disappears?

Directed versus undirected

PageRank is calculated on the original directed hyperlink graph. Shortest paths, centrality comparisons, node removal, and robustness use the undirected giant component so that connected-component fragmentation is well-defined.

The knockout experiment

For each of the 277 characters in the original giant component, we copied the same graph, removed exactly one node, and measured the new giant component, component count, and nodes outside the giant. The fragmentation score is the number outside the new giant divided by the original giant-component size.

Figure 1. Single-node knockouts ranked by structural damage.

Spider-Man causes the largest single-node fragmentation in this experiment, followed by Black Widow (Natasha Romanova), Doctor Strange, and Deadpool. This is evidence about the graph's counterfactual structure, not about what would happen to fictional storylines.

Does betweenness predict fragility?

The Pearson correlation between betweenness and fragmentation is 0.723; the Spearman rank correlation is 0.306. These values describe association, not proof that betweenness causes damage. The scatterplot makes the disagreements visible: some bridges are replaceable, while some nodes cause disproportionate damage relative to their centrality.

Figure 2. Betweenness versus single-node fragmentation.

Attack the Marvel universe

We started every strategy from the same original giant component. Degree, betweenness, and closeness use static rankings computed once. Adaptive betweenness recomputes after every removal. Random removal is the mean of 100 fixed-seed runs with a one-standard-deviation band.

Broker — or just popular?

We generated 200 degree-preserving undirected rewires. Every shuffle preserves each node's degree, so the null asks whether a character's betweenness is unusual beyond degree alone. A positive z-score means more broker-like than the rewired baseline; it does not mean “more important” in the real Marvel universe.

Figure 4. Degree versus betweenness z-score under the degree-preserving null.

Takeaway

The network has several kinds of “fame.” Degree counts links, closeness captures reachability, betweenness captures shortest-path brokerage, and PageRank rewards incoming links from central pages. The knockout experiment adds a robustness perspective: the most central-looking character is not automatically the one whose removal fragments the network most. The degree-preserving null is the comparison that keeps the interpretation honest.
