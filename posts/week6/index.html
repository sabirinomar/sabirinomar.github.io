Week 6 · Text + graph

Textually close but structurally far away

We asked a simple question: when two Marvel character pages are textually similar, how far apart are they in the hyperlink graph? The answer is not always “next to each other.”

On this page Question What we did What we found Evidence in the text Limitations Takeaway
The question

Question

Who is textually close to another character but still sits several steps away in the Marvel network?

01 / Compare the two views

What we did

We used the course’s 303-character Marvel graph and the plain-text corpus in the local data folder. For every pair of pages, we computed a TF-IDF cosine similarity from cleaned page text and a shortest-path distance from the directed character network. We then filtered out pairs with overlapping character-name tokens so the result would not be dominated by obvious name matches like “Spider” or “Blue.”

This leaves a cleaner question: do two pages sound alike even when their graph positions are not close? The analysis is deliberately simple and case-driven: a single narrative question, one main figure, and one real pair that we read in context.

03 / Read the text directly

Evidence in the text

Backhand (character)

“The character is usually depicted as a member of the Strikeforce: Morituri.”

morituri strikeforce process

In the page text, Backhand is tied to the team’s purpose, the deadly Morituri process, and the short lifespan that comes with it. The language is not just a generic hero description; it is the story of a team that gains power only by accepting a fatal cost.

Scatterbrain (Morituri)

“The character is depicted as a member of the Strikeforce: Morituri.”

morituri horde effect

Scatterbrain is described as a volunteer in the Morituri program, a person transformed by a scientific process that grants power while also leading to an inevitable death. The page shares the same central vocabulary: team, process, threat, and fatal consequence.

One caveat

Limitations

The graph and the text are not perfectly synchronized in time: the network reflects the course snapshot, while the page texts are the local corpus extracted for the analysis. That is fine for a case study, but it means the result is descriptive rather than causal. A shared team, plot arc, or editorial framing can explain both the similarity and the hyperlink structure.

The short version

Takeaway

Text and structure do not move in lockstep. Backhand and Scatterbrain are not adjacent in the graph, yet they are strongly aligned in the language they use because they belong to the same story world and the same fatal superhuman program. The real lesson is not that one model is “better” than the other, but that the two views capture different kinds of closeness.
